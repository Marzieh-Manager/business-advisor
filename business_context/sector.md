# Wholesale coffee roasting — how the category actually works

## What kind of business this is

A wholesale roaster is a **capacity-and-cash business wearing the costume of a
taste business.** Quality wins accounts; roaster hours, green-bean stock and
payment terms decide whether those accounts are profitable.

Three numbers matter more than revenue:

1. **Gross margin per kilo, per account** — tiered pricing, free machines and
   delivery costs can make a large account the least profitable one.
2. **Roaster utilisation** — how many of the available roasting hours are used.
   A second roaster before the first is full is capital spent on idle time.
3. **Cash conversion** — days between paying for green beans and being paid by
   cafes. Green beans are bought in bulk and paid for early; cafes pay late.

## Where the profit actually sits

- **Account retention.** A cafe on a standing order is worth years of revenue;
  losing one usually costs more than winning a new one earns.
- **Equipment and service.** Machine placement and servicing lock in accounts and
  carry their own margin, if minimum volumes are enforced.
- **Retail subscriptions.** Higher margin per kilo than wholesale, and predictable.
- **Training and education.** Barista training defends the account and is hard for
  a price-only competitor to match.

## The traps of this specific model

- **Statement terms are lending.** Every cafe on 14-day terms is financed by the
  roaster. The standard controls: a credit limit per account, an ageing report
  read weekly, and a fixed rule for what happens when an account is overdue.
- **Free machines without enforced minimums.** A loaned machine in a cafe that no
  longer meets its volume is a cost, not an asset.
- **Owner as backup for everything.** When the owner covers roasting or dispatch,
  sales stop - and sales are the only thing nobody else can do.
- **Buying from memory.** Without a written reorder point per green-bean line,
  stockouts of the most popular coffee are a matter of time.

## What separates roasters that scale

- **They hire or train a backup before they need one.**
- **They write down standards** - roast profiles, reorder points, credit rules.
- **They separate the weekly operating rhythm from the slow strategic one.**
- **They measure what they claim to care about** - account churn, on-time
  delivery, roaster hours used.
