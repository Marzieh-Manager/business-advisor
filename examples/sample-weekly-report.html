Biggest issue

Running out of your most popular coffee is now a pattern, not bad luck

A cafe on a standing order chose you because the coffee is the same every week. When their single origin is missing twice in a year, the risk is not one lost order - it is the cafe starting to taste a competitor's coffee.

Green-bean buying is currently done from memory. That works until the week you are busy, which is exactly the week it fails.

Evidence, in your own words
  • The Ethiopia ran out again, second time this year
  • Three cafes had to switch blends
◆ Recommendation for Harbourline

Write a reorder point for your five best-selling green-bean lines this week: the stock level at which you place the next order, based on your weekly kilos and the importer's lead time. Put it in the roast schedule spreadsheet Priya already uses, so the check happens every Friday without you.

Best opportunity

The office bundle could turn your machines from a cost into a product

You already place machines in offices and already deliver beans to them. Charging for the two together as one monthly bundle makes the revenue predictable and makes the machine pay for itself.

It also answers the price pressure: an office paying for a service is not comparing your price per kilo.

◆ Recommendation for Harbourline

Do not build it yet. Offer it to three existing office accounts as a simple monthly price for beans plus one service visit. If one says yes, you have proof; then set it up as a Shopify subscription.

What you may be avoiding

Three of this week's problems are one problem - you are the only backup

When Priya was off, you roasted all day and two sales calls did not happen. The stockout happened in a week you were busy. The second-roaster question keeps coming back, but the roaster is not the bottleneck - your time is.

Every role in the business falls back on you. Until someone else can roast a standard batch, any absence turns into lost sales.

Training a backup roaster ← Missed sales calls + Unplanned stockouts
Leadership lesson · for next time

The lesson from Wednesday is not that Priya was sick - it is that there was no plan B

Absence is normal. What was in your control was whether anyone else could run a standard roast. Right now nobody can, so the cost of any absence lands on your sales time.

A business grows when the owner's hours go to the work only the owner can do.

◆ Prepare for next time

Ask Priya to write down the roast profile for your two main blends and to train Tom on one standard batch per week for the next month. Next time someone is away, Tom roasts and you keep your calls.
